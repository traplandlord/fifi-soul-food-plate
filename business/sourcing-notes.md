## 2. Bulk buying tips

### Chicken
- **Default to drumsticks** for cost control (USDA FOB ~$0.54/lb Sep 2026; Depot retail often ~$0.59–$0.99/lb).  
- Treat **wings as a premium mix-in**, not the whole plate — Depot ~$1.87/lb vs Walmart retail often **>$3.50/lb**.  
- Buy what you will cook within **1–2 days** unless you have freezer space and a thaw plan.  
- Weigh a sample of 10 pieces once; write “avg oz/piece” on a kitchen card so portioning stays honest.  
- **Plan:** 2 pieces/plate ≈ 0.55–0.70 lb raw for drums-heavy builds.

### Collard greens
- Fresh: expect **heavy cook-down** — budget ~**0.75–1.2 lb raw per 4–6 oz cooked serving**.  
- Trim stems aggressively for tenderness; weigh **after** cook for portion ladles.  
- If fresh price spikes, switch to **frozen chopped** mid-week without changing the menu name; keep one “from fresh” market day for branding if you want.  
- Canned seasoned greens: drain/rinse to taste, finish with your own smoke/onion/vinegar so they taste “Fifi,” not can.

### Cornbread
- For markets: bake **same morning**; hold wrapped or in dry compartment.  
- Jiffy: cheap and fast for scale tests.  
- Scratch: move here when you want thicker Southern crumb and you’re baking full pans anyway — cost stays in the **$0.15–0.30**/square band.  
- Cut uniform squares (use a dough scraper) so every plate looks the same in photos.

### Oil & breading
- Filter oil between batches; log hours of use.  
- Pre-mix dry dredge in cambros; don’t portion salt blindly mid-rush.  
- Allocate **~$0.20–0.40**/plate for oil in your head until you measure actual usage over 3 cook days.

### Waste control
- Sell “last call” plates 30 minutes before close (same price or $1–2 off) rather than packing unsafe leftovers.  
- Freeze only raw chicken or uncooked greens components if your permit/process allows — not plated hot meals for next-day reheat unless EH-approved.

## 3. Packaging recommendations (heat + market)

### Best everyday plate pack
**9" × 9" 3-compartment microwaveable hinged container**  
- ~**$0.24–0.27**/each in case of 150 (Webstaurant Choice/Ecopax class).  
- Layout: large well = chicken; one small = greens; one small = cornbread.  
- Keeps cornbread from sitting in pot liquor.

### Alternate / chafer-friendly
**Aluminum foil pan (9×6" or 7–9" round) + lid** — Uline examples:  
- 9×6" S-25388 → **~$0.42**/with lid (250 ct @ $105).  
- 7" round S-25386 → **~$0.30**.  
Use when holding pans in a chafer and plating to order, or when hinged stock runs out.

### Avoid for hot fried chicken (usually)
- Thin clear salad-style 3-comp that warps with heat (check temp rating; some PS containers are not ideal for very hot fry).  
- Prefer PP / mineral-filled PP rated for hot foods (often up to ~275°F on Webstaurant specs).

### Guest kit (every plate)
1. Wrapped fork/knife/(optional spoon) + napkin — Depot kit.  
2. One wet wipe.  
3. Optional: mini hot sauce or house seasoning packet once brand is ready.

**Target disposables total:** ~**$0.40–0.50**/plate mid.

## 4. Heat / hold for car & market

| Scenario | Gear | Practice |
|----------|------|----------|
| Car hop (≤60–90 min) | 2× insulated delivery bags (Uline ~$30–33) | Load hot, minimize lid opens, probe check before first sale |
| Farmers market (2–5 hrs) | Insulated pan carrier **or** chafer with fuel + water pan | Keep product **≥135°F**; stir greens; rotate chicken so edges don’t dry |
| Trade show | Same as market + backup Sterno + thermometer | Power may be limited — fuel chafer is more reliable than electric |
| Plating at booth | Hotel pans in carrier; assemble hinged boxes to order | Crispier chicken than pre-boxing everything 3 hours early |

**Do not** use “parked car with windows up” as your only hot-hold method for multi-hour service.

**Thermometer rule:** Check every 30–60 minutes; write temps on a paper log (inspectors and your future self will thank you).

## 5. LA / SoCal vendor notes (high-level — not legal advice)

| Topic | Practical note |
|-------|----------------|
| Cottage food | **Cannot** cover fried chicken / hot plated meals. CDPH approved list is for non-PHF foods only. |
| MEHKO | Likely path to cook in a home kitchen in **opt-in counties** (LA County has a program). Expect application + annual fees; confirm current amounts with EH. Some cities (e.g. Pasadena, Long Beach, Vernon) may be separate. |
| Farmers markets | Market manager + often a **Temporary Food Facility** permit / coordination with county EH. Bring COI if required. |
| Selling from a car | Parking, vending, and health rules vary by city — ask EH *and* local code enforcement before advertising a pin drop. |
| Tax | CDTFA seller’s permit for taxable sales. |
| Always | Local Environmental Health has the final word for *your* address and sales model. |

Useful starting links:  
- CDPH Cottage Food: https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DFDCS/Pages/FDBPrograms/FoodSafetyProgram/CottageFoodOperations.aspx  
- LA County MEHKO: https://opportunity.lacounty.gov/mehko/

## 6. First shopping list (≈25–30 plates test)

**Protein**
- [ ] 18–25 lb chicken drumsticks (Depot case share or 4–5× 5 lb grocery bags)  
- [ ] Optional: 5–10 lb wings for mix plates  

**Sides / bake**
- [ ] Fresh collards for ~30 servings **or** 6–8 cans Glory 27 oz **or** ~15× 12 oz frozen  
- [ ] 5–6 boxes Jiffy (or scratch ingredients for 30 squares)  
- [ ] Eggs, milk/buttermilk, oil/butter  

**Fry / season**
- [ ] Flour, cornstarch (optional), salt, pepper, paprika, garlic, cayenne, onion  
- [ ] Fry oil (enough for deep or deep-shallow fry + 1 change)  

**Pack / serve**
- [ ] 1 case 9×9 3-comp hinged (150) — will last multiple events  
- [ ] 50–100 utensil kits + wet wipes  
- [ ] 2 insulated bags  
- [ ] Probe thermometer, gloves, foil, labels  

**Rough cash outlay for this test [E]:** **$180–350** depending on whether chicken is Depot case vs retail and whether packaging is full case.
